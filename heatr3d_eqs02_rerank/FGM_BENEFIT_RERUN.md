# FGM benefit re-run under the corrected `qrf_gradient="masked"` default

Follow-on to the EQS-02 shape re-ranking campaign (`RERANK_REPORT.md`). Generated by `build_fgm_report.py` from `fgm_shards/*.json`; the numbers below are not edited by hand.

## What is being tested

The published heatr3d FGM maps were built by `make_fgm(res)`, which inverts `res.T_phi90` -- a temperature field produced by the **legacy** Q_rf stencil. The EQS-02 correction therefore hits the FGM claim twice: the map DESIGN was derived from a legacy temperature field, and the benefit was EVALUATED under legacy physics. Five arms per shape, all n = 64, `phase_update="enthalpy"`, `make_fgm(magnitude=1.0, baseline=0.5, bpp=2)` (the published settings, identical in `run_cone_dumbbell_fgm_fixed.py` and `run_3d_study.py`):

- **A** -- legacy baseline (uniform dopant, legacy Q)
- **B** -- masked baseline (uniform dopant, masked Q)
- **Cl** -- legacy-designed map, LEGACY physics (old-regime reproduction)
- **C** -- legacy-designed map, masked physics (as-published map, corrected world)
- **D** -- masked-designed map, masked physics (honest go-forward)

Decision-relevant deltas: **C vs B** (does the as-published map still help in the corrected world?) and **D vs B** (what is the real achievable benefit?). `Cl vs A` is the in-campaign reproduction of the OLD regime, included so the published percentages have a like-for-like anchor.

## Method / cost

Three EQS solves per shape, five thermal marches. The baseline solve serves arms A and B (same V, two Q post-processings, identical total absorbed power); the legacy-designed-map solve serves Cl and C the same way; the masked-designed map needs its own solve because its gamma differs. Every march runs through `run(qrf_override=...)`, which skips the internal EQS solve. `heatr3d.py` was imported read-only; nothing under `dissertation_materials/` was written.

## Observations (read off the tables above, nothing inferred)

1. The baseline itself moves in BOTH directions under the correction: sigma_T rises for cone, dumbbell and falls for sphere, cylinder. Every FGM percentage is therefore quoted against a baseline that itself changed.
2. The in-campaign old-regime reproduction (Cl vs A) lands within 1.0 point of every published percentage (sphere -53.4 % vs published -54.0 %, cone -31.7 % vs published -31.9 %, dumbbell -43.2 % vs published -44.2 %), so the arms are anchored to the published setup despite the enthalpy scheme change.
3. The as-published map still reduces sigma_T under masked physics on 4/4 shapes (sphere, cone, dumbbell, cylinder), but by a smaller margin than in the old regime on every one of them.
4. Re-designing the map from the masked baseline beats the legacy-designed map on sphere, cone, dumbbell; it is WORSE on cylinder. The cylinder case is the outlier: the masked-designed map gives +0.3 % (null) while the legacy-designed map gives -23.4 % -- inverting the already-fairly-uniform masked T field produces a weaker correction than inverting the surface-hot legacy field. Not explained here; flagged.

## Limits (stated, not hedged)

1. The published claims were produced with `Params()` defaults, i.e. `phase_update="apparent_cp"`; every arm here uses `"enthalpy"` (the S1 energy-conserving scheme), as the rerank campaign did. So `Cl vs A` is a like-for-like reproduction of the OLD REGIME inside this campaign, not a bit reproduction of the published percentage.
2. Arm C's map is designed from THIS campaign's legacy-Q enthalpy `T_phi90`, not loaded from the published dopant volume. The map-similarity column above quantifies how close the two are for cone and dumbbell.
3. One grid (n = 64) and one FGM setting (magnitude 1.0, baseline 0.5, 2 bpp). No grid-convergence or magnitude sweep was run.
4. sigma_T here is the 3-D `std(T_phi90)` over part voxels (`heatr3d.Result.sigma_T`); it is not comparable to any 2-D `ui_rms*(T_bar-23)` number.
5. `cylinder` has no published FGM claim; it is carried as a control.

Total compute: 1.68 h summed over shards (4-way parallel, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`).
